Method

How the index is built

Exposure. Daytime land surface temperature from 31 cloud-screened Landsat 8/9 scenes (June–August, 2019–2025, 30 m), averaged where people live using 2020 census-block population; plus night-time surface temperature from MODIS Aqua (about 1:30 a.m.).

Sensitivity. Share aged 65+ and under 5, any disability (ACS 2020–2024), and adult heart disease, COPD, asthma, diabetes, stroke and frequent mental distress (CDC PLACES 2025).

Low adaptive capacity. Poverty, no vehicle, older adults living alone, limited English, no insurance, no internet, threat of utility shut-off, pre-1940 housing, distance to the nearest 2026 cooling centre, and lack of tree cover.

Index. Each of 21 indicators is converted to a percentile within Baltimore; the three components are averaged with equal weight. A principal-components version and a geometric mean rank tracts almost identically.

Uncertainty. 1,000 Monte Carlo draws propagate Census margins of error, PLACES confidence intervals, year-to-year temperature variation and random component weights.

Validation. Aggregated to 20 ZIP code areas, the index is compared with the CDC Heat & Health Index percentile of heat-related EMS activations reported to NEMSIS, 2020–2022, which was not used to build it.

Sources: USGS Landsat Collection 2 and NASA MODIS (via Microsoft Planetary Computer); ESA WorldCover 2021; CDC PLACES 2025; U.S. Census Bureau ACS 2020–2024 and TIGER 2020; CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 and Heat & Health Index 2024; Mapping Inequality (University of Richmond); Baltimore City Health Department cooling centres 2026; BNIA Community Statistical Areas; NOAA RCC-ACIS station data; live forecast from api.weather.gov.

Satellite surface temperature measures roofs and pavement, not the air. Health values are model-based estimates. This atlas describes neighbourhoods, not individuals. In a heat emergency call 911; for Baltimore cooling centre information call 311.
