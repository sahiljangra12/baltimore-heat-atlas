Method

How the index is built

21 indicators, ranked within Baltimore, averaged into three components with equal weight. Full details, code and data are on GitHub.

Exposure

Daytime surface temperature from 31 cloud-screened Landsat 8/9 scenes (June–August 2019–2025, 30 m), averaged where people live; night-time surface temperature from MODIS Aqua (about 1:30 a.m.).

Sensitivity

Aged 65+ and under 5, any disability (ACS 2020–2024); adult heart disease, COPD, asthma, diabetes, stroke and frequent mental distress (CDC PLACES 2025).

Low adaptive capacity

Poverty, no car, older adults living alone, limited English, no insurance, no internet, utility shut-off threat, pre-1940 housing, distance to a cooling centre, and lack of trees.

Uncertainty and validation

1,000 Monte Carlo draws of data error and weights. Checked against heat-related EMS activations (NEMSIS 2020–2022, via CDC Heat & Health Index) in 20 ZIP areas.

Baltimore Heat Atlas

By Sahil, MSE Data Science, Johns Hopkins University, October 2026. Paper: “Where Heat Meets Vulnerability: A Validated, Uncertainty-Aware Heat Vulnerability Index for Baltimore City's Census Tracts.”

Paper (PDF) · Code and data on GitHub

Satellite surface temperature measures roofs and pavement, not the air. Health values are model-based estimates. The atlas describes neighbourhoods, not individuals. In a heat emergency call 911; for cooling centre information call 311.
